StoryEditor

What it is and how it works

A single-file, offline editor for interactive "choose your own path" stories. You add pages and choices in your browser, the editor checks that every page and ending can be reached, shows the story as a Story Page Map, and exports each story as one small game file anyone can play online or offline. The whole editor is one HTML file that runs in your browser; the story stays in your browser storage as you type.

Why it exists

Writing a branching story is easy; knowing it works is hard. A choice can lead nowhere, an ending can need a key nobody can pick up, and a plain text file warns you about none of it. The Story Editor lets someone who does not program write such a story, be told in plain words what is wrong, and hand over one file with no server, account or install.

Works with Twine and your devices

Imports Twine 2 HTML, Twee 3 and Twine 1 stories, turns SugarCube and Harlowe code into rules and tells you in plain words what could not carry over. Runs in Firefox, Chrome, Edge and Safari on computers, Android and iPhone or iPad. Exported games run in somewhat older browsers still.

No ads, no accounts, no network

No ads, no sign-in, no tracking and no data sent anywhere. The editor is not allowed to make a network connection at all, so your story never leaves your device, and everything works offline.

One file that runs almost anywhere

The whole editor is one 298 KB HTML file. Nothing to install, no administrator rights and no special computing knowledge: open it in your browser. It fits phones, tablets and computers at almost any screen size, and a finished game is one small file too, about 25 to 50 KB.

Open source, with a quick tour

MIT licensed and free to use, change and share. The first time you open the editor a short spotlight tour shows the main areas; replay it from Options, Take the tour, or read the Quick guide inside the editor.

Exact checker

Tries every combination of items, numbers and dice to find dead ends and unreachable endings.

Story Page Map

Several views, from a plain flow to a bird's-eye shape of the whole story, with your pages, choices and routes drawn as a picture you can search and click.

Why it is this fast

These numbers are no accident. A significant effort went into making the story engine fast, so that it keeps up as stories grow. As games become far more massive, their mechanics more sophisticated, their visuals richer, and their playthroughs wildly different, logical and fun paths, the engine is built to scale with that complexity instead of slowing under it.

Objects and states

Items come in two kinds, and a story can use both anywhere.

Objects

Something the player carries or can hand over: a key, a wallet, a lantern. Picking one up, using it up and being asked for it are all plain choices.

States

Something the player is or has done: sick, brave, exhausted, "Met the archivist", "Alarm raised". A state is on or off.

Used everywhere

Both can show or hide a paragraph or a choice, be gained or lost when a page opens or when a choice is picked, and be shown to the player ("Carrying", "Feeling") or kept secret.

Greyed-out choices

A locked choice can stay visible, dimmed, with your reason ("You need the brass key"), so players know something is missing.

Advanced features

Options, then Advanced, has one switch per tab. With all four off the editor shows only pages, choices, objects, states and a simple Story Page Map; turning one off only hides it, and a story that uses it keeps working.

Write

Choices that give or take an object or state the moment they are picked, with no extra page.

Items

Numbers (health, courage, money) with their own range, dice checks that use them, and Givens: questions the player answers before page one ("Who are you?") so text and even item names differ per player.

Story Page Map

Choke-point spine, lanes per Given answer, story shape, simulated-player flow and arc views; coloring by ending odds, playtest heat or one item. Flow, tree and matrix are always there.

Game

Reader options for the exported game: back-button rules, endings found, a place code, the text-size button, odds and colors, with a live preview of your own story.

A checker that plays every route

The checker does not skim your story. It plays it with exactly the rules the finished game uses, from page one, and explores every situation a player could be in: the page, what they carry, what they are, every number value that matters and every dice outcome. Within its limit nothing is skipped.

Problems

Choices that lead nowhere, dead ends, pages nobody can reach, places a player gets stuck, loops they can never leave, endings no player can get to.

Warnings

Missing words, text or choices that can never appear, identical choices side by side, items nobody can obtain or that change nothing, a choice that takes something the player never has.

Endings and routes

Every ending, whether it can be reached, and the shortest way there. Why? explains in plain words what is missing, and Test from here replays a route with real values.

Honest limits

It explores up to 200,000 situations; past that it says it did not finish and never calls something impossible it has not proved. Up to 30 tracked items use fast numbers, more use exact big numbers with no limit.

Numbers are not tracked value by value: values the story can never tell apart share one situation, so a number tested only against 6 costs 7 situations, not 11. It counts every number, roll and locked choice too, and tells you about dice checks that can never pass or never fail and thresholds no player can reach. The 316-page example, with 12 keys and four states, finishes with no problems and every ending reachable.

Try an example story

Two stories ship with the editor. Play either as a reader would, or open the story in the editor and see how it was made.

The Keeper's Light

You step off the supply boat onto Gull Rock to relieve the lighthouse keeper for the winter, and the great lamp is dark. It is a small mystery with eight endings, built from four objects and three states: a lamp that needs oil, a shore with something on it, a cliff you may not want to climb.

A typical playthrough: the shortest ending takes 3 choices (about a minute); most take 10 to 14 choices, about 7 minutes; the longest takes 22, about 11 minutes. 34 pages, 8 endings.

Play The Keeper's Light

The Drowned Archive

You cross a tidal causeway to the Meridian Archive, a building that floods twice a day. Its keeper has stopped answering letters and its clock has stopped at ten past three. Why you came, as an heir, an inspector or a thief, changes what the Archive says to you; the tide rises as you wander, a dice check decides the risky climb, and the keeper, Ines Varga, will only help someone she trusts.

A typical playthrough: the quick endings take 1 to 6 choices (1 to 4 minutes). The two good endings take 21 to 25 choices even by the shortest route, about 9 to 11 minutes, and most readers wander and take longer. 51 pages, 8 endings, and it plays differently for each of the three visitors.

Play The Drowned Archive

See the Story Page Map

The editor draws your story as a map you can search, filter and replay. Lengths above are counted from the checker's shortest routes at about 200 words a minute plus a few seconds per choice; they are estimates, not timings of real readers.
